# `llm` eval, 2026-10-20, `@cf/zai-org/glm-4.7-flash`

- **Model:** `@cf/zai-org/glm-4.7-flash` on Cloudflare Workers AI (free allocation). Called through the research Worker's `/api/llm`, so it used the same quota checks as the live site, with thinking turned off and the Worker's default temperature (0.6).
- **Mode:** `llm`. The LLM writes all four stages, evidence included. No OpenAlex lookup happens before the stages run, which is the point: this measures what the model makes up on its own.
- **Sample:** the first 10 of the 20 interests, not all 20. A single run cost about 105 neurons, so all 20 would have come to roughly 2,100. The daily pool is 10,000 neurons, shared with the live site, and I wanted the eval to stay small. I made two calls with the same settings and merged them: `--offset 0 --limit 1` (to measure the cost) and `--offset 1 --limit 9`.
- **Cost:** 1,022.29 neurons in total (105.35 + 916.94), about 102 per run.
- **Raw:** [`2026-10-20-glm-4.7-flash-llm.json`](./2026-10-20-glm-4.7-flash-llm.json)

```bash
LLM_PROVIDER=free LLM_BASE_URL=https://research.ledring.dev pnpm eval --mode llm --offset 0 --limit 10 --out evals/results/<date>-glm-4.7-flash-llm.json
```

## What this says

- The pipeline holds up. All 10 runs parsed against the stage schemas and had a valid structure: at least 3 paths, the selected path exists, and every evidence id resolves.
- The citations don't hold up. Not one of the 60 was verified. 55 of them pointed at something that isn't a DOI or an OpenAlex work, so the checker can't confirm them. 4 were DOIs that don't exist, and 1 was a real DOI attached to a different paper's title. **If you leave this model to come up with sources itself, you can't trust them.** That's why the live site fetches evidence from OpenAlex first. For comparison, the `worker` mode on 2026-10-17, using the same model, verified 100% of its citations on the first 5 interests.
- On-topic evidence came out at 0.55, against 0.88 for `openalex` and 0.85 for `worker`. The model's paper titles drift away from the interest.
- 10 runs isn't many. The per-run spread is wide (on-topic goes from 0.167 to 1.0), so the averages are rough.
